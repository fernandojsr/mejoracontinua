Germany

Primary DACH track
CityAirport & transportJob market fitHousing reality
Frankfurt
Top pick
 Germany's busiest airport (~63M pax, 300+ destinations), Lufthansa's main hub, plus a major rail hub with ICE connections nationwide. Deepest English-friendly market: finance, consulting (Big Four), logistics/aviation, corporate ops. 2nd most expensive, but growth has slowed — one of the calmest of the Big 8 right now.
Munich 2nd busiest airport, fastest-growing major hub, more pleasant to navigate than Frankfurt. Strong corporate HQs (Siemens, Allianz, Infineon) plus a real digital-transformation scale-up scene (Celonis, Personio). Most expensive rental market in Germany, ~0.3% practical vacancy.
Berlin Regional/European hub — strong for short-haul, weaker for long-haul than Frankfurt or Munich. Most English-friendly work culture in the country, deep startup/remote-first scene. Cheapest of the Big 8 core cities, but a segmented, competitive search for newcomers.
Stuttgart / Hamburg / Cologne Regional airports, good rail links. Stuttgart: automotive/industrial (Mercedes, Bosch, Porsche) — good manufacturing fit. Hamburg: logistics/trade. Cologne: media/insurance. Hamburg and Cologne both tightening fast (+5–6% YoY rent growth); Stuttgart calmer.

Spain

Secondary / lower-effort track
CityAirport & transportJob market fitHousing reality
Madrid
Top pick
 Spain's largest airport, 3rd busiest in the EU, 200+ destinations, key European gateway to Latin America. Deepest market for consulting, corporate ops, finance, digital transformation — nearly every major multinational has its Spanish HQ here. Mid-pack: cheaper than Barcelona, pricier than Valencia.
Barcelona 5th busiest EU airport, strong European network, some recent operational strain (strikes, capacity). Tech/deep-tech hub (22@ District), biotech and software-heavy — a narrower fit for a generalist ops profile than Madrid. Priciest of the three.
Valencia Growing regional airport, well short of Madrid/Barcelona's global scale. Smaller market overall, but the Castellón ceramics cluster nearby is a genuine industry match — see below. Cheapest of the three by a wide margin (~25–35% under Madrid/Barcelona).

Castellón ceramics cluster — the closest industry match anywhere in this whole search

60–75km north of Valencia city. 80% of Spain's ceramic tile manufacturers are based here — ~60,000 jobs, over 20% of the province's GDP, and Spain is the world's second-largest tile exporter.

This lines up directly with the Uriarte Talavera background: production management, the QA department built from scratch, 5S implementation, raw-material standardization. Same industry, different ceramic tradition.

Watch: Porcelanosa, Pamesa Cerámica, Keraben, Grupo STN, Torrecid, Esmalglass, Baldocer. Spanish is the working language here, which is a non-issue given native fluency — but this track sits closer to a return to manufacturing leadership than to the "digital transformation" positioning built for DACH.
